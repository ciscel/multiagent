# Demo Speech — "No Internet" Offline Agent
Total run time: ~2 min 15 sec. Read naturally, don't rush the intro — the
error screens are doing work, let them land.

---

## 1. Intro (~30 sec) — over the three error screens

*[Show Blue Screen of Death]*

Remember this? Windows had a memory error so common it became a meme
before memes existed. Then memory protection shipped — and the Blue
Screen basically disappeared. Not a prettier error. A retired one.

*[Show Chrome's offline dinosaur]*

You've seen this one this week. "No internet connection." Something
broke, here's a dinosaur instead of a fix.

*[Show Twitter's Fail Whale]*

Every platform gets its error screen eventually. It's a rite of passage.
But notice — none of these three tell you what to do. They tell you
something broke. That's it. A dead end dressed up as information.

If something is truly agentic — truly disruptive — it doesn't just
display the error better. **It retires it.**

Today, we're retiring one of the biggest ones: **404. "No internet."**

---

## 2. What this is (~15 sec)

This isn't three products. It's one box.

An L1 tech-support agent. A network security system. A compliance tool.
Same engine. Zero internet required to run.

---

## 3. Why that matters here, specifically (~15 sec)

Lemma built their company to catch AI agents that fail silently — agents
that look like they worked and quietly got it wrong.

Ours never fails silently. Every decision comes with a citation to the
exact rule that made it. No guessing, no black box — a verdict you can
audit.

---

## 4. Live click-through (~60 sec)

*[Open the admin panel and the customer portal side by side]*

38% of every support call to an ISP starts with "no internet." Today
that's a guess made blind over the phone — reset your remote, reset
your router. This box replaces the guess with a diagnosis.

*[Click Carrier Outage]*

Watch the customer screen. No call. No hold music. It already knows —
outage's upstream, no truck roll, no action needed.

*[Click Local Glitch]*

Here it's not just diagnosing — it fixed it. Auto-remediated, customer
told, done.

*[Click Hardware Fault]*

This is the one case that actually needs a technician. And that's the
point — the box tells the difference. Most support calls today can't.

*[Click Neighbor Relay Available]*

No internet, no cellular, no subscription. If a neighboring unit is
reachable, the status gets relayed peer to peer. This box doesn't need
its own uplink to get the word out.

*[Click Security Anomaly]*

Same engine, different question. An unrecognized device shows up on the
network, matches a known attack pattern, gets isolated automatically —
no human in the loop.

*[Click "View OSCAL Evidence Export"]*

And here's the third piece. Every one of those events — connectivity,
security — lands in the same structured, cited evidence trail. That's
not a dashboard. That's something a business can hand straight to their
cyber insurer.

Support agent. Security system. Compliance tool. One box, one loop,
never needs a connection to do any of it.

---

## 5. Close (~10 sec)

If something is truly agentic, truly disruptive, it has to retire an
error code.

**Today, we say goodbye to 404.**

---

## Delivery notes

- Don't read this word for word — know the beats, say them like you mean
  them. The error-screen intro is the hook; don't rush past it.
- Pause after "today, we're retiring one of the biggest ones" before
  saying "404" — let the room fill in the guess for half a second.
- Same pause before the final line. It's the whole pitch in one sentence
  — let it sit.
- If a judge asks a question mid-demo, answer it, then land on the close
  line anyway before you finish your turn. Don't skip it.
